Zongo Falls

West of Kinshasa, in Kongo Central province, the Inkisi River drops about 65 metres over a broad rock ledge in a roar of brown water and mist. Zongo Falls is one of the most spectacular sights in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and it is close enough to the capital to be done in a day. It is a long day, and the road is part of the experience, but the reward is a waterfall you can hear before you see.

This guide covers what Zongo Falls is, what you will see around it, the smaller falls downstream where you can swim, where to eat and sleep, and what to arrange before you leave Kinshasa.

What Zongo Falls is

A big river, a hard drop and a lot of spray.

Zongo Falls (in French, Chutes de Zongo) is a waterfall on the Inkisi River, a tributary of the Congo. Most sources give its height as about 65 metres, and the water spreads across a wide, stepped ledge before dropping into a gorge. The spray is so thick it makes its own rain around the falls, so expect to get soaked at the viewpoints and to see rainbows when the sun is out.

The falls sit beside a hydroelectric installation, and a lodge on the riverbank gives day visitors somewhere to eat and rest. Further downstream the Inkisi runs on towards the Congo River, and tour operators run short excursions to the smaller Sunguza falls and to the point where the two rivers meet.

The falls and the river

Power from above, rapids below.

The main falls are the headline, but the river is worth as much of your time as the drop itself.

The main drop

Water pours over a broad ledge in several steps and roars into the gorge. Keep well back from wet rock.

Mist and rainbows

The spray rises into a permanent cloud. In sunshine it often catches a rainbow, so bring something to protect your camera.

The Inkisi rapids

Below the drop the river runs fast between forested banks, with white water over rocky shelves.

Right at the falls

The rock platforms in front of the main drop make for dramatic photos, but the spray is heavy and the stone is wet.

At the viewpoint

Visitors gather at the rocky viewpoints to watch and film the falls.

Into the mist

On a grassy bank above the gorge, the spray rises like smoke.

Things to do

Look, listen, walk, eat.

  1. Stand in front of the falls

    Walk to the viewpoints over the main drop. The sound alone is worth the journey. Wear shoes with grip, because rock near the spray is slick.

  2. Swim at the Sunguza falls

    Roughly 10 km downstream, about a 20-minute drive or hike beyond the main falls, smaller cascades drop onto flat rocks. You can lie under the water for a natural massage, and unlike the main falls it is reported to be safe to swim. Ask at the lodge about a guide.

  3. See where the rivers meet

    Operators also run visits to the beach where the Inkisi joins the Congo River. Ask whether it fits your day.

  4. Have lunch by the river

    Seli Safari Resort has a restaurant beside the falls and is the usual lunch stop for day visitors.

  5. Stop at Kisantu on the way back

    The Kisantu Botanical Garden, founded in 1900 and one of central Africa's oldest, is a pleasant detour if you are staying overnight and have a relaxed return.

The massage falls

Smaller cascades downstream, where you can stand or lie under the falling water for a natural massage. Unlike the main falls, they are reported to be safe for swimming.

A sandy riverside beach

A broad sandy beach beside the water, good for a rest between falls. Operators also run visits to the point where the Inkisi meets the Congo River.

Pool and shade

Seli Safari Resort has a pool and garden for cooling off after the walk to the falls.

Stay the night

Seli Safari Resort offers chalets, villas and bungalows, so you can see the falls in the quiet of early morning.

Where to stay

A riverside lodge with a pool and a restaurant.

Seli Safari Resort sits on the banks of the Inkisi beside the falls, with chalets, villas and bungalows among tropical forest. It has a restaurant and a pool and runs on solar power and a generator. Staying the night means you see the falls in the quiet of the early morning and skip the long drive back the same day. Book ahead, especially at weekends and in school holidays.

Bedroom

A comfortable room with a balcony looking into the trees.

Bathroom

Plan your visit

Leave early, bring cash and go with a 4x4.

Before you go. Bring plenty of drinking water, sun protection, shoes with grip, swimwear for the Sunguza falls, a full change of clothes (the spray soaks you at the viewpoints) and waterproof bags for phones and cameras. Carry cash in both Congolese francs and US dollars, and fill the tank before leaving the city. Stay well back from wet rock and from the edge, as the spray makes it slippery. Tell someone your plan, and check your government's current travel advice for DR Congo. If you have an extra day, Bombo-Lumene and Lola ya Bonobo make good separate outings.

Frequently asked questions

How far is Zongo Falls from Kinshasa?

About 130 to 135 km by road, in Kongo Central province. Sources quote anything from 3 to 6 hours each way, depending on traffic and the state of the final stretch.

Can you do Zongo Falls as a day trip from Kinshasa?

Yes, and many people do, but it is a long day. Leave very early to beat the traffic out of the city. If you want time at the falls, a meal and no rush back, an overnight stay nearby is more comfortable.

Do I need a 4x4 to reach Zongo Falls?

A 4x4 with a driver is strongly recommended. The first half of the journey is on reasonably good road, but the second half is rough.

How high are Zongo Falls?

Most sources give about 65 metres (213 ft). The falls are on the Inkisi River, a tributary of the Congo.

Can you swim at Zongo Falls?

Not at the main falls, where the water is powerful and the rocks are slippery. The smaller Sunguza falls, about 10 km downstream, are reported to be swimmable.

When is the best time to visit Zongo Falls?

The falls are at their fullest and loudest in the rainy season, with January often recommended, but the road is harder then. In the dry season the water is lower and the track easier.

Is there somewhere to eat or stay at Zongo Falls?

Yes. Seli Safari Resort sits on the Inkisi River beside the falls, with a restaurant, a pool and chalets, villas and bungalows. Book ahead, especially at weekends.
