Odzala-Kokoua National Park

Gorillas in forest clearings, elephants at the river’s edge and some of Africa’s wildest rainforest. Here’s what to see, where to stay and how to get there from Brazzaville.

Odzala-Kokoua covers about 13,500 square kilometres of the north-west Republic of Congo, in the heart of the Congo Basin. It was first protected in 1935, which makes it one of Africa’s oldest national parks, and it has been managed by African Parks in partnership with the Congolese government since 2010.

The landscape is a mosaic of rainforest, swamp forest, rivers and open clearings called bais, where gorillas, elephants and antelope come to feed on minerals and sedges. It is remote, uncrowded and reached by air, which is a big part of its appeal.

Wildlife

Gorillas, elephants and forest secrets

Odzala-Kokoua is best known for western lowland gorillas, with some of the highest densities recorded anywhere, and for forest elephants. Animals are often easiest to see at the bais, while much of the wildlife stays hidden in dense forest, so a good guide and some patience count for more than luck.

Western lowland gorillas

Critically endangered apes that live in family groups led by a silverback. The park holds one of the largest populations in Africa, and camps run guided treks to habituated groups.

Silverbacks

Adult males often step out into forest clearings to feed on herbs and grasses, giving rare open views of a gorilla from a safe distance.

Forest elephants

Smaller than savanna elephants, with straighter tusks. They follow forest trails to mineral-rich bais and rivers, and are most often seen at the edge of clearings and water.

Elephants by the camp

Elephants can pass close to camp, so follow your hosts’ guidance after dark and keep a respectful distance.

Sitatunga

A shy swamp antelope with splayed hooves for soft ground, often spotted at the edge of a bai around dawn and dusk.

Leopard

Leopards live here but are rarely seen, so a sighting is a bonus. The park protects most of Central Africa’s mammals, though not lions.

Things to do

On foot, from the deck and by the fire

Activities are led by local guides and follow the rhythm of the forest: early walks, quiet hours at a bai and long evenings in camp.

Gorilla trekking

Follow trackers into the forest to spend a limited time with a habituated gorilla group. Treks vary in length and can be muddy, so bring sturdy boots.

Watching a bai

Sit quietly at the edge of a clearing or on an elevated platform and watch gorillas, elephants and antelope come and go.

Swamp-forest walks

Wade through flooded forest with a guide, looking for birds, monkeys and signs of elephants. Expect to get wet.

Evenings by the fire

Camps look out over the clearings, and evenings are spent on the deck sharing the day’s sightings.

Where to stay

Camps in the forest and beside the bais

Accommodation is limited to a handful of thatched, timber-built camps inside and beside the park. Camp Imbalanga is run by African Parks in the north-east, while Odzala Discovery Camps offers Ngaga, Mboko and Lango. Numbers are small, so book well ahead.

Getting there

Starting from Brazzaville

Odzala-Kokoua is reached from Brazzaville, where visitors complete immigration formalities. Camps arrange a scheduled charter flight of about two hours to Mboko airstrip, usually on Mondays and Thursdays, and the park can also be reached by road via Mbomo on its southern edge. Confirm the current schedule with your camp. The driest months are June to September and December to February. A visa and a yellow fever certificate are required, and malaria precautions are essential. See the Brazzaville guide for where to stay before and after your trip.

Plan your trip: access, weather and health

Checked November 2026. Camp rates and availability change, so get current prices from each camp before you plan.

  • Getting there. Guests normally fly by charter from Brazzaville to an airstrip in the park. An overland route exists but takes days. Camp Imbalanga, run by African Parks, is also reachable by the national road. Book the flight with your camp.
  • Camps. Camp Imbalanga has four bungalows and offers guided walks and river trips with local guides. Ngaga, Lango and Mboko are run by Odzala Discovery Camps and have around six rooms each, so total capacity is small. Rates are set by each camp and are not published here, so ask them directly.
  • Weather. This is rainforest. Annual rainfall is above 1,500 mm and rain falls on over 100 days a year, so pack waterproofs and expect mud in any month. Ask your camp what each season is best for.
  • Health. Yellow fever vaccination and malaria prophylaxis are standard advice for Central Africa. Check Republic of Congo entry rules for your passport and see a travel clinic well before you fly.
  • Conservation. African Parks manages the park with the Congolese government under a 25-year agreement, and your visit helps fund local employment and anti-poaching work.
