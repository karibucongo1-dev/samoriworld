Barcelona

Gaudí's wild buildings, medieval lanes and a seafront within a metro ride. Here's how to see the best of Barcelona in a few well-paced days.

Why go

Barcelona packs Gaudí's fantasy buildings, a medieval Gothic Quarter, lively markets and city beaches into a compact, walkable city. Book the big sights ahead, then leave room for long lunches and unplanned wandering.

Where to Stay in Barcelona

Most first-time visitors stay in the Eixample or the old town. Pick by what you want on your doorstep: Gaudí buildings, medieval lanes or the seafront.

Kimpton Vividora Barcelona

In the Gothic Quarter, a couple of minutes from La Rambla and a short walk from the cathedral. A rooftop pool and the Terraza de Vivi bar make it a lively base for old-town sightseeing.

Hotel Casa Fuster

A Modernista landmark by Domènech i Montaner at the top of Passeig de Gràcia, in the Gràcia district. Elegant lounges, close to La Pedrera and a quieter, more residential feel than the old town.

Majestic Hotel & Spa Barcelona

A grand five-star hotel on Passeig de Gràcia, among the designer shops and a short walk from Casa Batlló and La Pedrera. Polished service, a spa and easy metro and train links.

Eurostars Grand Marina Hotel

A five-star hotel in the World Trade Center at Port Vell, a short walk from La Rambla and close to the cruise terminals. Sea views and a seasonal rooftop pool.

What to See in Barcelona

Group sights by neighbourhood: Gaudí in the Eixample, the old town on foot, and the Palau de la Música a few streets away. Book timed tickets ahead for Sagrada Família, Casa Batlló and La Pedrera.

Gaudí and Modernisme

The Sagrada Família is Gaudí's unfinished basilica, and its central Tower of Jesus Christ was completed in 2026, making it the tallest church in the world. Book online well ahead. Casa Batlló and La Pedrera show his work on Passeig de Gràcia, and the Palau de la Música Catalana is a Modernista concert hall with a stained-glass ceiling.

La Boqueria — The market off La Rambla is open Monday to Saturday, 8 am to 8:30 pm, and closed on Sundays. Go early for the best produce, and eat at the counters or in the quieter streets nearby.

Park Güell, Montjuïc and Camp Nou — Gaudí's hillside park has a timed-ticket monumental zone with mosaic walls and arches. On Montjuïc, the Magic Fountain runs light shows on an evening schedule that changes by season, so check before you go. Football fans can tour Camp Nou, the home of FC Barcelona; check current visitor access before you plan.

Eating in Barcelona

Expect seafood and rice dishes, tapas, grilled vegetables and Catalan wine. Locals eat lunch around 2 pm and dinner after 9 pm, so book ahead for dinner.

Rice dishes and rooftop dining

Order an arròs, rice cooked in a wide pan with seafood, meat or vegetables, and share it. For a rooftop lunch, La Terraza del Claris, the terrace restaurant of Hotel Claris in the Eixample, is a bright spot above the city.

Getting to Barcelona

Barcelona-El Prat Airport (BCN)

The airport is about 14 km south-west of the centre. The Aerobús reaches Plaça Catalunya in around 35 minutes, the R2 Nord train runs to Sants and Passeig de Gràcia, and the L9 Sud metro links both terminals to the network, usually with a change. Taxis are metered.

A Simple 4-Day Route

Day 1

Old Town

The Gothic Quarter and La Boqueria in the morning (closed Sundays), El Born for tapas at dusk.

Day 2

Gaudí's Eixample

Casa Batlló and La Pedrera on Passeig de Gràcia, then the Palau de la Música Catalana.

Day 3

Sagrada Família and Park Güell

Sagrada Família at your earliest slot, then Park Güell in the afternoon with a timed ticket.

Day 4

Montjuïc and the Beach

Montjuïc by day, a seafood rice lunch by the sea, and the Magic Fountain if the schedule fits.

Good to Know

Getting around

The metro and buses cover most of the city, and the old town and Eixample are walkable. A multi-day transport card saves money on short stays.

Tickets and timing

Book Sagrada Família, Casa Batlló, La Pedrera and Park Güell online in advance, especially in spring and summer. Dress modestly for the basilica.

Costs and etiquette

Spain uses the euro. Watch your pockets in crowded areas such as La Rambla and the metro, and expect dinner late, often after 9 pm.
