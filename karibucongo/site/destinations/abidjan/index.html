Why go

Abidjan wraps a modern skyline around a lagoon, with rainforest inside the city limits and long Atlantic beaches within easy reach. Four or five days is enough for the Plateau's towers and bridges, the markets and museums, a day on the sand at Grand-Bassam, and a few long, very good dinners.

Where to Stay — Abidjan

From a landmark lagoon-side classic to modern business-district towers, these four make good bases. Plateau puts you near the offices and sights; Cocody has the lagoon views.

Sofitel Abidjan Hôtel Ivoire

The city's landmark hotel, set in lagoon-side grounds in Cocody with a big pool and mid-century architecture. Come for the setting even if you only stay for a drink.

Pullman Abidjan

A polished, central tower in the Plateau business district with a stylish restaurant and quick access to offices, museums and the lagoon.

Novotel Abidjan

Reliable mid-range comfort with big windows and skyline views. A sensible pick if you want a calm base without a luxury price tag.

Noom Hotel Abidjan Plateau

A contemporary design hotel in Plateau with warm, modern suites and high-floor views over the business district.

Plateau, Faith & Culture

Start in the Plateau, where the skyline, the cathedral and the mosque all sit within a short ride of each other.

Saint Paul's Cathedral & Salam Mosque

Two of Abidjan's boldest buildings

Saint Paul's is a modernist landmark with a huge cross rising over sweeping terraces, and its hilltop views are worth the visit alone. The Salam Mosque adds a tall minaret and carved lattice windows. Dress modestly and check visiting hours before you go.

Museum of Civilisations — masks, sculpture and regalia from Côte d'Ivoire's many peoples, with bilingual French and English labels.

Treichville Market — a big, busy market for fabrics, crafts and street life. Go in the morning and bring small notes.

Beaches & Green Escapes

The best of Abidjan is the way it opens onto water and forest, often less than an hour from downtown.

Grand-Bassam & the Lagoon

Atlantic surf and still-water calm

Grand-Bassam, a UNESCO-listed former colonial capital, is the classic day trip, with a wide Atlantic beach, beach shacks and grilled fish. The Atlantic currents are strong, so swim only where locals do. For something quieter, a lagoon-side escape trades surf for flat water and canoes.

Banco National Park

Real rainforest inside the city, with shaded trails and towering trees. Go early, wear closed shoes and bring water.

Bingerville Botanical Garden

A calm, shady half-day just outside the city, with long palm avenues and plenty of room to wander.

Dahliafleuve Nature Reserve

A bamboo-arched path and quiet river scenery for a slower, greener day out. Confirm access and any guide fees in advance.

Eat, Drink & Play

Abidjan's food scene is a big part of the draw, from lagoon-side grills to white-tablecloth dinners.

Le Bistrot du Palmier — a stylish dining room of brass, timber and velvet. Book ahead for dinner.

Saakan Restaurant — a vivid wax-print ceiling and a contemporary take on Ivorian flavors, ideal for a special night.

Happy Park Akwaba — pools, slides and inflatables. A fun day out if you're traveling with kids.

Getting There

Félix-Houphouët-Boigny International Airport

Airport code: ABJ

Abidjan's main airport sits about 16 km south-east of the Plateau, roughly 30–45 minutes by car depending on traffic. Air Côte d'Ivoire, the national carrier, connects the city with much of West and Central Africa, and many international airlines fly in from Europe and beyond.

A Simple 4-Day Route

Day 1

Plateau

Saint Paul's Cathedral, the Salam Mosque and the Museum of Civilisations, then sunset by the Alassane Ouattara bridge.

Day 2

Markets & Dinner

Morning at Treichville Market, an easy afternoon, then a long dinner at Saakan or Le Bistrot du Palmier.

Day 3

Grand-Bassam

Drive east for the old colonial quarter and a beach lunch, and back before the evening traffic.

Day 4

Green Day & Departure

Early walk in Banco National Park or the Bingerville gardens, then onward to the airport.

Good to Know

Getting around

Ride-hailing apps and metered taxis are the easiest way around, and traffic is heavy at rush hour, so plan around it. Lagoon ferries and buses are cheap but slower. Agree taxi fares before you set off.

Entry requirements

Most visitors need an e-visa arranged online before travel, and proof of yellow fever vaccination is required. Rules change, so check the official government portal and your own country's advice before booking.

Packing

Light, breathable clothes, sunscreen, repellent and a compact rain jacket. Carry some cash in CFA francs for markets and small restaurants, and modest layers for religious sites.

Before you go: entry and health

Checked November 2026. Entry rules depend on your passport, so confirm them with the Ivorian authorities before you book.

  • Visa. Most visitors need a visa and apply online through the official e-visa portal before flying. Reports say the e-visa is valid only if you arrive at Félix-Houphouët-Boigny Airport (Port-Bouët), not at a land border. Check the portal for your nationality, since some passports are exempt.
  • Yellow fever. A valid yellow fever vaccination certificate is mandatory for entry and is checked on arrival. Get vaccinated at least 10 days before you travel and carry the yellow card.
  • Other documents. Carry a passport valid for at least six months, a confirmed hotel booking or host address, and a return or onward ticket.
  • Malaria. Malaria risk is high across Côte d'Ivoire. Ask a doctor or travel clinic about prophylaxis before you go.
  • Getting to Grand-Bassam. It is a day trip from Abidjan. Ask your hotel for the current taxi or shared-car price rather than relying on a figure from a guide, and agree the fare first.

Sources: Côte d'Ivoire e-visa portal · CDC yellow fever · UNESCO: Grand-Bassam
