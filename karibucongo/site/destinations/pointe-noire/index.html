Pointe-Noire is the economic capital of the Republic of the Congo and its second-largest city, with around 1.4 million people. It was chosen in 1923 as the Atlantic terminus of the Congo-Ocean Railway and is still the country’s main port and oil hub — yet with its beaches, seafront restaurants and relaxed pace it often feels more like a seaside town.

This guide covers where travelers actually stay, where to eat seafood by the shore, what’s worth seeing in the city — from the Côte Sauvage to the cathedral and the African Circle museum — and the trips further out to Diosso Gorge, Pointe Indienne and Conkouati-Douli National Park, plus how to get here by air or from Brazzaville.

Good to know: entry requirements

Pointe-Noire is in the Republic of the Congo (Congo-Brazzaville), a different country from the DRC. Visas, entry rules and border procedures change, so check the current requirements with the embassy or the official immigration authority before you travel.

Things to do

In the city

A long Atlantic beach, a landmark cathedral, a museum of African masks and a boat trip to see whales — Pointe-Noire is easy to explore in a couple of days.

La Côte Sauvage

The city’s best-known beach — a long Atlantic stretch of golden sand and rolling surf, with seafront restaurants and beach stalls. The currents can be strong, so swim with care, avoid the beach alone after dark and watch for broken glass underfoot.

Cathédrale Notre-Dame-de-l’Assomption

The principal Catholic church of Pointe-Noire, recognizable by its tall perforated bell tower and round rose window. It is an active place of worship, so dress modestly and ask before photographing services.

Musée du Cercle Africain

A collection of African masks, sculpture and painting, with a souvenir shop on site — a good place to learn about Central African art before you buy in the markets. Check opening hours locally.

Downtown and Avenue Charles de Gaulle

The center mixes French-colonial influences with busy boulevards, bars and restaurants. For a more local evening, head to La Cité, the city’s African district, and the Grand Marché.

Whale watching

Boat trips from Pointe-Noire head out to see humpback whales and dolphins in the open Atlantic with a naturalist guide. Whales are seasonal, so confirm dates and the operator before you plan around it.

The port

Congo's gateway to the Atlantic

Pointe-Noire is the Republic of the Congo's only seaport and the sub-region's only deep-water port with direct access to the sea. It handles containers, bulk goods and petroleum, which is why the city is the country's economic capital. The port is a working, secured area, so you will not normally be able to walk in, and you should ask before photographing it.

Congo Terminal

Seen from above, red quay cranes load and unload ships at the deep-water berths while rows of stacked containers fill the yard behind. The terminal has operated under a concession since 2009. It handled more than 1.2 million containers (TEUs) in 2025, and an expansion is planned.

Ships from around the world

Large container ships berth here beside the quay cranes, including LNG-powered vessels such as the CMA CGM Scandola. The port sits on shipping routes linking the Americas, Europe and Asia, with tugboats guiding the big ships alongside.

Day trips

Out of the city

Pointe-Noire is also a base — these are the trips worth the drive. Roads and conditions vary, so confirm access and a local guide before you go.

Diosso Gorge

A canyon of red, pink and orange cliffs cutting through forest, about half an hour north of the city and often called the Grand Canyon of the Congo. Hire a taxi for the day; expect a small local fee at the viewpoint, and local guides can lead walks down to the bottom.

Pointe Indienne

A sandy beach about 19 km north of the city that fills with locals at weekends, with hotels and restaurants nearby — an easy day at the coast.

Conkouati-Douli National Park

A large, little-visited park near the Gabon border, roughly five hours’ drive north in the dry season, with forest, lagoon and savanna. Confirm park access, accommodation and guides before leaving Pointe-Noire.

Bela Falls

Spectacular falls deep inland — much closer to Brazzaville than to Pointe-Noire, so they fit better on a Brazzaville itinerary. Read the Brazzaville guide.

Getting there

Landing at Agostinho Neto

Most flights arrive at Agostinho Neto International Airport (PNR), about 5 km southeast of the city center — roughly 15 to 30 minutes by taxi, depending on traffic. Airlines serving it have included Ethiopian Airlines, Turkish Airlines and Air France. Schedules change, so compare current fares before you book.

From Brazzaville

The Congo-Ocean Railway (CFCO) links Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire over roughly 515 km, and buses run the same corridor. Many travelers fly instead — the train and roads can be slow and unreliable, so check current timetables, conditions and security before you rely on them.

Travel advisory: allow extra time overland

Overland journeys between Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire can take far longer than planned, especially in the rainy season when roads deteriorate. Travel in daylight, carry water and food, and ask your hotel or a local operator for current advice.
