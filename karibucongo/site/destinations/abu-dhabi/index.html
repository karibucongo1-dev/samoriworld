Why go

Abu Dhabi spreads outward rather than up — across islands, corniches, and desert edges — and rewards a walking pace. Most trips split into two halves: a day or two on culture and heritage in the city centre, then a run out to Yas Island for the parks, or into the dunes for a night under real stars. Four or five days is enough to get a genuine taste of both.

Where to Stay — Abu Dhabi

Pick a base by which half of this guide you're prioritising — corniche and culture, or Yas Island and the parks.

The St. Regis Abu Dhabi

A gold-domed corniche address, five minutes from Qasr Al Watan. Good base for the culture half of the trip.

Emirates Palace Mandarin Oriental

The city's most iconic address, and a landmark in its own right even for non-guests.

Conrad Abu Dhabi, Etihad Towers

Steps from the Observation Deck at 300, with marina views and easy access to both halves of the city.

Le Royal Méridien Abu Dhabi

City-centre value, a short walk from the Central Market and Al Itihad Square.

Hilton Abu Dhabi Yas Island

The natural base for SeaWorld, Ferrari World and Yas Mall — palm-lined pools, kids in tow.

Abu Dhabi, Before You Head Out

City-centre landmarks worth seeing before you head to Yas Island or the dunes.

Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque

Eighty-two domes and the world's largest hand-knotted carpet. Free entry, modest dress required.

Qasr Al Watan

A working presidential palace that opens its library dome and great hall to the public.

Qasr Al Hosn

The city's oldest standing building — a coral-and-limestone fort. Free entry.

Heritage Village

A recreated Bedouin settlement on the breakwater — free entry, modest by design.

Culture & Heritage

Abu Dhabi's museums and monuments trace the emirate from pearl-diving fort to global art capital, often within a few minutes' drive of each other.

Louvre Abu Dhabi

Jean Nouvel's perforated dome scatters sunlight across the courtyard like light through palm fronds. Inside, the collection moves chronologically and geographically at once, threading a single human story across civilizations rarely displayed side by side. Closed Mondays.

Al Itihad Square — the giant dallah coffee pot monument, a five-minute stop and a good orientation point downtown.

Jubail Mangrove Park — a boardwalk through one of the oldest mangrove forests on the coastline. Kayak tours available.

Yas Island: Parks & Family Fun

A twenty-minute drive from downtown, Yas Island packs theme parks, an aquarium, and a Formula 1 circuit onto one reclaimed island.

SeaWorld Abu Dhabi

Built around research rather than performance, its 11-million-litre aquarium is ringed by walkways where manta rays pass close enough to count their spots. The coral-lit dining hall turns lunch into part of the exhibit.

Yas Mall — 370+ stores and a cinema, a useful pressure valve between theme parks.

Al Maryah Island — downtown's answer to Yas Mall, closer to the corniche hotels.

Desert & Souk

An hour past the city limits, the buildings give way entirely to red sand — a different pace to close out the trip.

An evening in the Liwa dunes

Camel trains still cross the dunes at golden hour, and desert camps lay out low majlis seating, shisha, and a barbecue dinner under a sky with none of the city's light pollution left in it. Book a sunset drive and stay for dinner.

Central Market Souk — air-conditioned corridors of cashmere, rugs, and gold.

Observation Deck at 300 — the whole coastline resolved into a single map. Go at sunset.

Getting There

Etihad Airways

The national carrier flies into Zayed International Airport, one of the region's largest terminals — its dune-inspired roof setting the tone before you've left baggage claim. Forty minutes from the corniche.

A Simple 5-Day Route

Days 1–2

City & Culture

Mosque, Qasr Al Watan, Qasr Al Hosn, and the Louvre.

Day 3

Yas Island

SeaWorld or Ferrari World, then Yas Mall for dinner.

Day 4

Desert & Souk

Morning at the Central Market, sunset safari and dinner in the dunes.

Day 5

Relax & Depart

Observation Deck at 300, corniche walk, and a flight home.

Good to Know

Getting around

Taxis are metered and everywhere; Careem and Uber both operate. A rental car is worth it for desert day trips.

Entry requirements

Many nationalities get a visa on arrival; check the official UAE government site rather than third-party lookalikes.

Packing

Modest dress for the mosque and palace, sun protection, and a light layer — malls and hotels run cold on AC.
