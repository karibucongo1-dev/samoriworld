Muanda sits on the Atlantic coast of Kongo Central, close to where the Congo River reaches the sea. It is a long way from the pace of Kinshasa: wide sandy beaches, fishing communities, and a river mouth lined with mangroves.

This guide covers the beaches, the Mangroves National Park, the sea turtle conservation work along the shore, the deep-water port taking shape at Banana, and where travelers stay and eat.

Things to do

Mangroves, turtles and the river mouth

Muanda's best experiences are outdoors, and most are shaped by the water.

Mangroves National Park

The DRC's only marine park, a 768 km² wetland protected since 1992 and recognised as a Ramsar site. Manatees, hippos and crocodiles live in and around the mangroves, so go with a local guide.

Sea turtle conservation

Conservation teams patrol nesting beaches and move threatened nests to protected hatcheries. Ask locally about the nesting season before you plan your dates.

The river mouth

Take a boat onto the wide estuary, where small island communities sit in water that is part river, part Atlantic.

Banana port, under construction

The DRC's first deep-water port is rising at Banana on this stretch of coast, with DP World as investor and Mota-Engil leading construction. It is a working site, so view it from a distance.

Getting there

Flying into Muanda

Muanda has its own airport (MNB), a short distance from the Atlantic coast, with scheduled flights from Kinshasa N'Djili. Schedules change often, so check current flights before you plan.
