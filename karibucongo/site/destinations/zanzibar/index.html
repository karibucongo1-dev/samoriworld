Zanzibar

Spice-island old town, sandbar picnics and a different kind of beach on every coast.

Why go

Zanzibar is really three trips in one. Stone Town is a walkable maze of coral-stone lanes, markets and old fort walls; the north coast around Nungwi and Kendwa has the white sand and sunsets; and the east coast, from Matemwe down to Paje and Michamvi, is where the tides, kitesurfers and beach restaurants live. Add boat days to sandbanks, reefs and Prison Island's giant tortoises and a week fills up easily. It is warm all year, but the island has two rainy seasons, so timing matters more than people expect.

Where to Stay

Pick a coast first, then a hotel. The north is for swimming at any hour; the east is quieter, windier and tidal; Stone Town is the base for history and food.

Gold Zanzibar Beach House & Spa

Kendwa, north-west coast. A boutique-style beachfront resort on Kendwa's white sand, which is not much affected by the tides, so you can swim at almost any time of day. Roughly 50 minutes by road from the airport.

The Mora Zanzibar

Muyuni Beach, Matemwe, north-east coast. A large all-inclusive resort of around 250 suites with three pools, on a quieter stretch of coast near Mnemba. Allow about an hour from the airport.

Diamonds Bijoux

Bwejuu, south-east coast. A beachfront all-inclusive with a private beach, two pools and a spa, close to Paje's kitesurfing and the Michamvi restaurants. Around 80 minutes' drive from the airport.

Stone Town and the Islands Off It

Darajani Market

Stone Town's main market: spices, fruit, fish and everyday goods in covered lanes. Go in the morning, carry small notes, and expect to bargain at the souvenir stalls.

Stone Town on foot

The old town is a UNESCO World Heritage site and best done slowly: fort walls, carved doors and the waterfront. A half-day is enough for the highlights, a full day if you linger over lunch.

Prison Island (Changuu)

A short boat ride from Stone Town. The draw is the colony of giant tortoises, some of them very old. Boat and entry are often priced separately, so check what your tour includes.

Nakupenda sandbank

A sandbar off Stone Town that changes with the tide, reached by boat. Usually combined with Prison Island on a half-day trip with snorkelling and a beach lunch.

On the Water

Zanzibar is as much a boat destination as a beach one. These are the trips worth planning a day around.

Turtle pond, Nungwi

The Mnarani conservation pond in Nungwi looks after rescued sea turtles. A small entry fee applies and it is an easy half-hour stop from the beach.

Dhow trip to Tumbatu

Sail out from the north coast on a traditional dhow with snorkelling stops, then visit Tumbatu island with a guide. Good in the late afternoon for the light.

Mnemba snorkelling and dolphins

Mnemba is the island's classic snorkelling and diving spot off the north-east coast. Dolphin sightings are common but never guaranteed, so choose an operator that keeps a respectful distance.

Full-day dhow adventure

Safari Blue-style trips spend a whole day on a dhow with snorkelling, a sandbank stop and a seafood lunch. Book with a licensed operator, through your hotel or a trusted agent.

Beaches: North vs East

The two coasts feel very different, so choose by how you like to spend a beach day.

Nungwi and Kendwa (north)

The classic Zanzibar beach: powder-white sand, lively sunsets and good swimming at any tide on the north-west side. Busiest area, with the widest choice of hotels, bars and dive centres.

Paje (east)

A long, windy beach that is Zanzibar's kitesurfing hub. At low tide the sea retreats far out, so swimming depends on the tide. Relaxed, café-heavy and popular with a younger crowd.

Where to Eat

Three beach-side spots on the east coast, all easy to combine with a day at Paje and Michamvi.

The Rock (Michamvi Pingwe)

The famous restaurant on a rock off Pingwe beach: you wade out at low tide and take a boat at high tide. Seafood-led menu. Book ahead, and check the tide when you reserve.

Hello Capitano (Paje)

A casual beachfront restaurant and coffee spot on Paje's kite beach. Easy lunches with the kites overhead, and good value by Zanzibar standards.

ZanziBarista (Paje)

An open-air beachfront restaurant and coffee house on the shore at Paje, with bean bags and a relaxed vibe. A good sunset-drinks stop between kitesurf sessions.

Getting There

Abeid Amani Karume International (ZNZ)

Zanzibar's only international airport sits a short drive from Stone Town, usually 15 to 20 minutes. Transfers to the north and east coasts take roughly one to one and a half hours, so book a pick-up with your hotel, and check bags are tagged to ZNZ if you connect through Dar es Salaam.

Via the Gulf

Dubai or Doha

Emirates and flydubai serve Zanzibar from Dubai, and Qatar Airways serves it from Doha, so one stop from most of Europe and Asia. Qatar's schedule has varied by season, so check current timetables. See our Dubai and Doha guides.

Via East Africa

Nairobi or Addis Ababa

Kenya Airways connects through Nairobi and Ethiopian through Addis Ababa, which suits trips that start in Central or West Africa. Pairing it with a safari? See our Kenya guide.

Via Turkey

Istanbul

Turkish Airlines flies between Istanbul and Zanzibar, a handy option from Europe with a long layover for city time. See our Istanbul guide.

A Simple 7-Day Route

Days 1–2

Stone Town

Land, Darajani Market, the old fort and waterfront, then Prison Island or Nakupenda by boat.

Days 3–4

North coast

Kendwa or Nungwi for swimming, the turtle pond, and a sunset dhow trip.

Days 5–6

East coast

Paje for lunch and kites, Mnemba snorkelling, and The Rock at sunset (check the tide).

Day 7

Fly home

Allow at least 90 minutes from the east or north coast to the airport.

Good to Know

Best time to go

June to October is the dry, cooler season and the most reliable for beaches. December to February is hot and mostly dry. The long rains fall from March to May, peaking in April, and many beach lodges close then; November brings shorter showers.

Entry requirements

Most visitors need a Tanzanian visa, which can usually be arranged online before you travel. Rules, fees and health-entry requirements change, so check the official Tanzania Immigration site and your own government's travel advice before booking.

Dress and tides

Zanzibar is majority Muslim: cover shoulders and knees in Stone Town and villages, and keep swimwear for the beach and resorts. On the east coast, check tide times before planning swims, boat trips or lunch at The Rock.
