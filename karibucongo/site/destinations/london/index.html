Why go

London runs east to west along the Thames — Big Ben and Westminster giving way to the dome of St Paul's, the fortress walls of the Tower, and the crowds of Trafalgar Square. It's a city built for walking, with a hotel scene anchored by a handful of names that have been hosting royalty, and everyone else, for well over a century.

This guide covers the landmarks travelers actually cross off, the hotels worth the splurge, and a short, honest list of where to eat in between.

Things to see & do

Cross the city on foot

From the Elizabeth Tower to the glass floor of Tower Bridge — the sights that anchor a first visit, and a couple that locals still send you to.

Westminster

Palace of Westminster & Big Ben

The seat of Parliament since the 11th century, with the Elizabeth Tower — Big Ben's actual name — chiming over the Thames since 1859.

Westminster

Westminster Abbey

Coronations since 1066, royal weddings, and the graves of seventeen monarchs — right next door to Parliament, but a different building entirely.

Westminster Bridge

Parliament from the River

The classic postcard angle — cross Westminster Bridge on foot, or catch it from a passing boat for the same view without the crowds.

St James's

Buckingham Palace

The King's official London residence. You can't go inside most of the year, but the gates, the memorial, and the crowds are a scene on their own.

Changing of the Guard

Changing the Guard

Usually 11am, alternate days — check the schedule before you go, since it doesn't run daily in the colder months.

South Bank

The London Eye

135 metres up, one slow half-hour rotation — book a sunset slot if you can, when the whole city turns gold.

Inside the Pod

A Ride in the Capsule

Each glass pod holds up to 25 people, so it's less private than it looks in photos — still worth it for the view over the Thames.

Charing Cross

Trafalgar Square

Nelson's Column, the fourth plinth's rotating art commission, and the National Gallery steps — London's unofficial town square.

The City

St Paul's Cathedral

Christopher Wren's dome has watched over the City since 1710. Climb to the Whispering Gallery if you don't mind 257 steps.

Southwark

Tower Bridge

Still a working bascule bridge — check the lift schedule online if you want to see it split in two for a passing ship.

Inside Tower Bridge

The Glass Floor Walkway

43 metres up, looking straight down through the floor to the traffic below. Not for the faint of stomach.

Tower Hill

Tower of London

Nine centuries of royal history and the Crown Jewels, guarded by Beefeaters who still live on-site.

Tower of London

A Tour with a Beefeater

Free with entry, and worth timing your visit around — the Yeoman Warders' tours are equal parts history lesson and stand-up routine.

Bloomsbury

The British Museum

Free entry, eight million objects, and the Rosetta Stone. Pick two or three galleries rather than trying to see it all in one go.

Southwark

Imperial War Museum

A Spitfire hangs in the atrium; the galleries below cover conflict from the First World War to today. Free to enter.

South Kensington

Natural History Museum

"Hope" the blue whale skeleton hangs over the main hall — one of the most photographed ceilings in the city, and free to visit.

Camden

Camden Town

Market stalls, vintage shops, and the canal — Camden's the least buttoned-up part of central London, and it shows.

Southwark

Borough Market

London's oldest food market, under a Victorian iron roof. Go hungry, skip breakfast, and graze your way through.

Fenchurch Street

Sky Garden

Free to visit if you book ahead — three floors of gardens and a 360° view from inside the "Walkie-Talkie."

West End

Piccadilly Circus

Neon screens, the Shaftesbury Memorial Fountain, and the gateway to Soho and the West End theatres.

Marylebone

Oxford Street

1.5 miles of flagship stores, from Selfridges to the high street names — best tackled on a weekday morning.

Marylebone

Oxford Street, Lit Up

The Christmas lights switch-on turns the whole street into an event — come back after dark from late November.

River-wide

The Thames by Uber Boat

The fastest way between the Tower, the South Bank, and Greenwich — and considerably cheaper than a sightseeing cruise.

After dark

The Thames by Night

Tower Bridge and the Shard both light up after sunset, and a handful of operators run evening cruises past them. It's a quieter, cheaper way to see the skyline than most of the paid viewpoints — and the photos hold up better than you'd expect.

A green afternoon

Hyde Park

350 hectares in the middle of the city — enough space for a proper walk, a rented rowboat, and a rose garden all in one visit.

Also worth knowing

The Waldorf's Palm Court

The Waldorf Hilton's glass-roofed ballroom is one of the most-booked wedding and event spaces in central London. If a date matters to you, get in early — good weekends fill more than a year out.

Where to eat

A short, honest list

London's dining scene is enormous — these are the four we'd send a first-time visitor to, across a few different price points and moods.

The Strand

Savoy Grill

Gordon Ramsay's take on the Savoy's Art Deco dining room. Leather booths, a menu that leans classic British, and Beef Wellington for two.

Charing Cross

Tandoor Chop House

Tandoor-fired sharing plates a few minutes from Trafalgar Square. Order more naan than seems reasonable — everyone does.

Hyde Park Corner

Afternoon Tea at The Lanesborough

Chandeliers, a glass atrium, and a tea list nearly as long as the sandwich menu. Book two to three weeks ahead.

Mayfair

CUT at 45 Park Lane

Wolfgang Puck's steakhouse inside the Dorchester Collection. Dry-aged beef, a serious wine list, and a room that stays lively late.
