Why go

Singapore packs an absurd amount into a small footprint — futuristic gardens next to a colonial shophouse district, a Michelin-starred hawker stall next to the world's priciest skyline pool. It's clean, it's easy, and it rewards even a short stopover.

Where to Stay — Singapore

From a colonial landmark on the river to a treehouse over the rainforest reservoir, here's where to base yourself.

The Fullerton Hotel

A former General Post Office turned grand hotel, right on the river near Merlion Park and the CBD.

Pan Pacific Singapore

A dramatic multi-storey atrium near Marina Bay, with glass lifts and a genuinely striking lobby.

Banyan Tree Mandai Rainforest Resort

Cocoon-shaped villas on stilts over the Upper Seletar Reservoir, next to the zoo and night safari.

Singapore, Before You Head Out

Eat at hawker centres

Some of the best food in the city is $5 plates at a hawker stall, not a restaurant.

Fly Singapore Airlines if you can

Consistently rated one of the world's best carriers, and it flies straight into Changi.

Book a candlelight concert

String-quartet shows lit entirely by candles pop up around the city — book ahead, they sell out.

Currency is SGD

Cards and contactless work almost everywhere, including most hawker stalls.

Gardens by the Bay

The Supertree Grove is free to wander at ground level and turns into a full light-and-sound show after dark. Pay for the OCBC Skyway to walk the bridge between the tallest trees, and set aside a separate few hours for the Cloud Forest and Flower Dome — the waterfall inside the Cloud Forest dome is worth the ticket alone.

Chinatown & Hawker Culture

Chinatown's shophouse streets are worth a slow wander in their own right, but the real draw is the food: Maxwell Food Centre and Chinatown Complex are packed with stalls that have held Michelin recognition for years, still serving plates for a few dollars. Go hungry and share plates across a few stalls rather than committing to just one.

An Evening Out

Rooftop bars over the CBD

Boat Quay and Clarke Quay have a run of rooftop bars looking straight down the river to the skyline.

A candlelight concert

Touring string ensembles play film and pop scores by candlelight in venues around the city.

Green Spaces & Southern Islands

Singapore is greener than its skyline suggests, and a short ferry ride puts you on a near-empty beach.

National Orchid Garden

Inside the UNESCO-listed Botanic Gardens, with thousands of orchid varieties on show.

St John's Island

A 30-minute ferry from the mainland gets you a quiet swimming beach with almost no crowds.

Kusu Island

A small pilgrimage island with a Chinese temple and Malay shrine, plus a calm lagoon beach.

Getting There

Singapore Airlines connects Changi to most major hubs directly, and the airport itself is a destination — Jewel's indoor waterfall and gardens sit right in the terminal, so a long layover here isn't a bad thing. Changi is consistently ranked among the world's best airports for a reason.

A Simple 3-Day Route

Day 1

Marina Bay & the Merlion

Walk the bay from Merlion Park to Marina Bay Sands, then head up to Gardens by the Bay for sunset and the Supertree light show after dark.

Day 2

Chinatown, hawkers & the river

Spend the day in Chinatown and Tiong Bahru, eat your way through a hawker centre, then catch the rooftop bars along the river at night.

Day 3

Green Singapore

Morning at the Botanic Gardens and National Orchid Garden, afternoon on the MacRitchie TreeTop Walk, or swap it for a ferry out to St John's or Kusu Island.

Good to Know

Getting around

The MRT is fast, cheap, and covers almost everywhere you'll want to go. Grab a contactless card or just tap a credit card directly at the gates.

Entry requirements

Many nationalities get visa-free entry for short stays — check current requirements for your passport before booking.

Packing

Hot and humid year-round — pack light, breathable clothing and bring a light layer for aggressively air-conditioned malls and the MRT.
