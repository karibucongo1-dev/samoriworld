Why go

Dubai builds bigger than almost anywhere else on earth, but the appeal isn't just scale — it's how close the record-breaking towers sit to open desert and old-town souks. Four or five days covers the skyline, the Palm, a desert evening, and enough downtime by the pool to make it feel like a holiday rather than a checklist.

Where to Stay — Dubai

From the world's most famous sail-shaped icon to family-friendly resorts on the Palm, here's where to base yourself.

Burj Al Arab

The world's most iconic sail-shaped hotel, set on its own private island. Book afternoon tea or dinner even if you're not staying, just to see inside.

Jumeirah Marsa Al Arab

A dramatic wave-shaped silhouette on the coast near Burj Al Arab, one of the newest luxury addresses in Dubai.

Hilton Dubai Palm Jumeirah

Beachfront on the Palm's crescent, with one of the best stretches of private sand on the island.

Grand Hyatt Dubai

A resort built around its own water park, tucked between Downtown and the Creek — a good pick for families.

Downtown & The Palm

The two halves most first-time visitors build a trip around — the world's tallest building, and the world's most photographed artificial island.

Burj Khalifa & The Dubai Fountain

The world's tallest building has an observation deck on the 124th–148th floors — book ahead, sunset slots sell out first. Time your visit for the evening so you catch the choreographed fountain show right below.

Palm Jumeirah & Atlantis — ride the monorail out for the view alone. Atlantis has a water park and aquarium open to day-trippers.

Dubai Marina Walk — a 7km promenade lined with restaurants and yachts, best walked once the heat breaks in the evening.

Desert & Design

Away from the towers, Dubai's other side: dunes carved into shapes visible from the air, and some of the most photographed architecture on Earth.

Love Lake & the Museum of the Future

A heart-shaped lake carved into the dunes near Al Qudra, free to visit and a good stop on the way back from a desert safari. Downtown, the Museum of the Future's silver torus — wrapped in Arabic calligraphy — is one of the most photographed buildings on Earth. Book a timed entry ticket ahead.

Getting There

Dubai International Airport

One of the world's busiest airports, with the Metro's Red Line running straight from the terminal into Downtown and the Marina. Get a Nol card on arrival — the Metro is clean, air-conditioned, and covers most of what tourists want.

A Simple 4-Day Route

Day 1

Downtown

Burj Khalifa deck at sunset, then the fountain show below.

Day 2

The Palm & Marina

Monorail to Atlantis, then an evening walk along the Marina.

Day 3

Desert Day

Dune-bashing tour with a stop at Love Lake on the way back.

Day 4

Design & Departure

Museum of the Future in the morning, flight home in the afternoon.

Good to Know

Getting around

The Metro is genuinely good and covers Downtown, the Marina, and the airport. Taxis are metered, cheap, and everywhere.

Entry requirements

Many nationalities get a visa on arrival; check the official UAE government site rather than third-party lookalikes.

Packing

Light layers for the heat, something smart-casual for Downtown malls, and swimwear for the Marina or a desert camp pool.
